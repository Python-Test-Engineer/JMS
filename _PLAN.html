Job Management System — Product Plan

What this is. A product and business plan for JMS, a CMS + job tracker for Acme Windows (a fictional window-installation company). It is deliberately written without code: it says what the business needs, who may do it, what the screens do, and how we will know each stage is finished.

What happens next. _TECH.md turns this into a technical specification — browser app, HTMX UI, Node service, a JSON document store first and SQLite/ Postgres later. Every requirement here is written so _TECH.md can answer it with a schema, a route, a template, or a test.

What "done" mimics. The web edition of this plan, and the app's own documentation pages, reuse the house explainer system already used by the mini-js-coder site and book: one self-contained HTML file, two-pane sticky contents with filter and scroll-spy, terracotta/serif house style, and the floating Aa appearance panel.

1. Product frame

1.1 Problem

Acme Windows runs on paper, spreadsheets and phone calls. Managers juggle a whiteboard of installations, engineers ring the office to say a job slipped, and customers ring the office to ask when someone is coming. Nobody can answer "what is happening next week, and with whom?" without three conversations.

1.2 Product in one sentence

JMS is one place where managers create customers and jobs, assign engineers and dates, engineers report what happened on site, and customers watch their own installation progress — with each person seeing only the parts of the app their role allows.

1.3 Goals (v1)

# Goal Success looks like
G1 Single source of truth for jobs Every scheduled job exists in JMS with dates, status and engineers
G2 Engineers self-serve their day An engineer opens JMS on a phone or laptop and sees only their jobs, with one obvious "update status" button
G3 Customers stop ringing the office A customer can log in and see status, dates and their documents for their own jobs
G4 Managers see the plan A week-forward timeline and workload view answers "who is where, when"
G5 Paperwork is generated, not typed Job sheet and invoice PDFs are produced from the same data
G6 Admins change access without a developer Role → action permissions are editable in the app and take effect immediately
G7 The app is comfortable to use Large type, large buttons, few steps, readable by office and field staff alike

1.4 Non-goals for v1 (explicitly deferred)

  • Email or SMS delivery of PDFs (download only in v1; the data model must not block it).
  • Online payments, card processing, accounting-system sync.
  • Stock/materials inventory, van tracking, route optimisation.
  • Multi-company tenancy (but every core table carries a company_id so it can be added).
  • Native mobile apps (responsive web only).
  • Customer self-registration (invite-only, created by staff).

1.5 Guiding principles

  1. One obvious next action per screen. Large primary button, plain verb.
  2. Visibility is not security. Hiding a pill is a convenience; every rule is enforced on the server on every request.
  3. The job is the centre of the universe. Customers, engineers, invoices, documents and events all hang off a job.
  4. Nothing is deleted, everything is archived and audited. History answers "who changed this and when".
  5. Data-driven permissions. Roles are rows, not if statements.
  6. Same design system as our published guides. No second visual language.

2. Actors, roles and permissions

2.1 Roles

Role Who they are What they care about Default land-on screen
ADMIN The system owner / office IT Access, correctness, configuration Admin dashboard
MANAGER Office staff who sell and schedule Pipeline, dates, engineers, money Operations dashboard
ENGINEER Field installers Today's jobs, status, notes My jobs
CUSTOMER Acme's clients When is my installation, what's happening My jobs (portal)

Each user has exactly one role in v1 (role assignment is per user, not per job). A job may have many engineers. A MANAGER may also carry field capability (can_engineer), so office staff who still install can be assigned to jobs without holding a second role.

2.2 Permission model (must be data-driven)

  • Permissions are named action keys in the form <area>.<verb>[.<scope>], e.g. job.create, job.view.assigned, invoice.view.own.
  • A role → action key → allowed matrix is stored in the database and seeded with the defaults below.
  • An Admin can toggle any cell except the safeguards listed in §2.5, and the change applies to the next request (no redeploy, no logout).
  • The UI renders navigation pills and buttons from the same matrix, so an action the user cannot perform is never shown.
  • Server-side authorisation is mandatory for every route and every HTMX partial; a hidden button is never the only defence.
  • Every matrix change is written to the audit log with before/after values.

2.4 Object-level scoping rules (hard rules, not toggles)

These are business invariants. Changing them would be a code change, not a permission cell:

  1. A Customer user resolves to exactly one customer_id; every query they make is filtered by it. They can never address another customer's job by ID.
  2. An Engineer sees only jobs where they have an active assignment, plus jobs they completed in the last 90 days (for "what did I do" questions).
  3. A Customer never sees: other customers, cost/price breakdowns of any job, company financial dashboards, engineer phone numbers or internal notes marked internal.
  4. An Engineer never sees: customer pricing, invoices, other engineers' schedules beyond their own jobs, admin screens.
  5. An Engineer may only advance status forward on their own assigned jobs. Overriding, reverting, cancelling or reopening is a Manager/Admin act.
  6. Managers and Admins see everything except the permission matrix and user administration, which are Admin-only by default.

2.5 Safeguards the matrix may not remove

  • At least one active ADMIN must always exist.
  • An Admin cannot remove their own admin.permissions.edit if they are the last Admin.
  • admin.audit.view cannot be granted to CUSTOMER or ENGINEER.
  • Every matrix edit is audited and cannot be silently reverted without a new audit row.

3. Domain model (conceptual)

_TECH.md will translate these into tables, keys and constraints. The model is stated in business language first, with the attributes v1 needs.

3.1 Entities

Company — id, name ("Acme Windows"), trading_name, email, phone, address, vat_number, logo_path, currency (GBP), timezone (Europe/London), default_theme.

User — id, company_id, email (unique, login name), password_hash, display_name, role_id, customer_id (nullable; set only for CUSTOMER), can_engineer (field capability; true for ENGINEER, optional for MANAGER), phone, status (INVITED / ACTIVE / SUSPENDED / ARCHIVED), must_change_password, theme_prefs (JSON: mode, type, fonts, colours), last_login_at, created_at.

Role — id, key (ADMIN, MANAGER, ENGINEER, CUSTOMER), label, description, is_system (system roles cannot be deleted, only re-permissioned).

Permission — role_id, action_key, allowed. Composite primary key.

Customer — id, company_id, name, contact_name, email (unique per company), phone, address_line1/2, town, postcode, notes, status (ACTIVE / ARCHIVED), created_at, created_by. A Customer is both a record and, optionally, a login (one User with the CUSTOMER role pointing at it).

Job — id, company_id, reference (human-readable, e.g. JOB-2026-0042, unique), customer_id, title, description, work_type (e.g. WINDOW_INSTALL, REPAIR, SURVEY), status (see §3.3), priority (NORMAL / URGENT), site_address (defaults to the customer's), planned_start, planned_end, actual_start, actual_end, price_net, vat_rate, price_gross (Manager-only; hidden from Engineer and Customer), internal_notes, customer_notes, created_by, created_at, updated_at, archived_at.

JobAssignment — job_id, user_id (field-capable: ENGINEER, or MANAGER with can_engineer), is_lead, assigned_at, assigned_by, removed_at (soft removal keeps history).

JobEvent — id, job_id, actor_user_id, type (STATUS_CHANGE / ASSIGNMENT / SCHEDULE_CHANGE / NOTE / DOCUMENT / PRICE_CHANGE), from_value, to_value, message, visibility (INTERNAL / CUSTOMER), at. This single log powers the job timeline, the customer-visible history and the audit trail.

Invoice — id, company_id, number (INV-2026-0007, gapless per year), job_id, status (DRAFT / ISSUED / PAID / VOID), issued_at, due_at, paid_at, net, vat_rate, vat, gross, notes, created_by. One job has at most one live invoice in v1 (re-issue = void + new).

Document — id, type (JOB_SHEET / INVOICE), entity_type, entity_id, version, generated_at, generated_by, file_path, checksum. Documents are regenerable; the row records that a PDF was produced and from which version of the data.

Setting — key, value (company defaults: VAT rate, invoice due days, default password policy flags, theme defaults, PDF footer text).

Session / AuthToken — server-side session record (_TECH.md decides cookie vs token), with expiry, user agent, and revocation for password resets.

3.2 Relationships at a glance

Company 1─n User          Company 1─n Customer 1─n Job n─n User (assignments)
Company 1─n Job           Job 1─n JobEvent        Job 1─0..1 Invoice
Role    1─n User          Role 1─n Permission     Job 1─n Document
Customer 1─0..1 User (the customer's login)

3.3 Job status machine

Status Meaning Entered by Allowed next
DRAFT Being prepared, not committed Manager, Admin SCHEDULED, CANCELLED
SCHEDULED Dates and at least one engineer set Manager, Admin IN_PROGRESS, ON_HOLD, CANCELLED, SCHEDULED (reschedule)
IN_PROGRESS Engineer on site / work started Engineer (own job), Manager, Admin COMPLETED, ON_HOLD
ON_HOLD Blocked (parts, weather, access) Engineer (own job), Manager, Admin IN_PROGRESS, CANCELLED
COMPLETED Work finished Engineer (own job), Manager, Admin INVOICED, IN_PROGRESS (reopen: Manager/Admin only)
INVOICED Invoice issued Manager, Admin PAID, COMPLETED (void invoice: Manager/Admin)
PAID Invoice settled Manager, Admin — (terminal)
CANCELLED Job abandoned Manager, Admin — (terminal)

Rules attached to the machine:

  • Leaving DRAFT requires planned_start, planned_end and ≥ 1 engineer.
  • Entering IN_PROGRESS stamps actual_start if empty; entering COMPLETED stamps actual_end. Manager/Admin may correct actual dates afterwards (job.status.override).
  • Every transition writes a JobEvent with actor, from, to and optional note.
  • A status a user may not set is not rendered for that user.

4. Workflows

Each workflow is written as a numbered path with a clear end state and the screens involved. _TECH.md should be able to derive routes, forms, validation and tests from these.

W1 — Add a customer and give them a login

  1. Manager opens Customers → New customer.
  2. Fills name, contact, email, phone, address (single column, large fields).
  3. Chooses Create customer login (on by default). Email becomes the login name; the default password JMS123 is set and flagged must_change_password.
  4. Saves → customer record created, User (CUSTOMER role) created, status INVITED, audit event recorded, confirmation shows the login email and the fact that the password must be changed at first sign-in.
  5. If the email already exists, the form explains which record uses it and how to open that customer instead (no duplicate customers, no silent merge).

End state: customer exists, can sign in, sees an empty "My jobs" page and a prompt to change their password.

W2 — Create a job

  1. Manager opens Jobs → New job (or New job from a customer page, which pre-fills the customer).
  2. Enters title, work type, description, priority, site address, price (Manager/Admin only), customer notes.
  3. Saves as DRAFT → job reference is allocated and shown immediately.
  4. The job page offers the next steps as large pills: Assign engineers, Set dates, Publish schedule.

End state: a DRAFT job with a reference that anyone can search for.

W3 — Assign engineers and plan dates

  1. On the job page, Manager opens Assign engineers (multi-select of active ENGINEERs, one marked lead).
  2. Sets planned_start / planned_end (date + time block: morning/afternoon/all day).
  3. Saves → status may move to SCHEDULED (system asks once, defaulting to yes).
  4. Booking conflict check: warn if an engineer already has an overlapping job; warning is advisory in v1, not a hard block.
  5. Engineers see the job under My jobs immediately, with a "dates set" notification marker in the app (no email in v1).

End state: job is SCHEDULED, appears on the timeline and in each engineer's list.

W4 — Engineer updates a job on site

  1. Engineer signs in on a phone → lands on My jobs, today first.
  2. Opens a job → big status control showing only the transitions they may take (Start job, Hold, Complete).
  3. Optionally adds a note and a customer-visible message.
  4. Tapping Start job sets IN_PROGRESS and stamps the actual start; Complete asks for a short completion note, then stamps the actual end.
  5. The job's timeline shows the change, and the customer sees the new status (and the customer-visible note) on their next visit.

End state: status, actual times and notes are recorded by the person who did the work, from the place where it happened.

W5 — Manager closes out and invoices

  1. Manager sees COMPLETED jobs needing invoices under Insights → Needs invoicing.
  2. Opens the job → Create invoice. Net/VAT/gross default from the job price and company VAT setting; invoice number is allocated.
  3. Saves as DRAFT, reviews the PDF preview, then Issue → status INVOICED, job moves to INVOICED, invoice PDF becomes downloadable to the customer.
  4. When payment lands, Manager marks Paid → job PAID.

End state: one invoice, one PDF, one audit trail, and a customer who can download the invoice without asking.

W6 — Customer checks progress

  1. Customer signs in (email + JMS123, forced to change on first login).
  2. Lands on My jobs: cards with reference, title, status pill, planned dates, assigned lead engineer's first name, and a link to details.
  3. Job detail shows a plain-language status, planned and actual dates, the customer-visible timeline, and a Documents list (job sheet, invoice).
  4. No prices beyond their own invoice, no other customers, no company dashboards.

End state: the customer's question is answered without a phone call.

W7 — Generate the job sheet and invoice PDFs

  1. Any user with document.job.pdf opens a job → Job sheet (PDF).
  2. The PDF is generated from live data on demand, branded with company name, address and logo, and stamped with the generation time.
  3. Invoice PDFs are downloadable once the invoice is ISSUED (or to Manager/Admin while DRAFT).
  4. If the job changes after generation, the next download reflects the new data; the Document row records version and time (no stale cached PDFs in v1).

End state: printable artefacts with zero retyping. Emailing them is a later milestone that only needs a delivery service, not a data change.

W8 — Admin adjusts what a role can do

  1. Admin opens Admin → Roles & permissions.
  2. Sees the role × action matrix from §2.3 with the current state, grouped by area.
  3. Toggles a cell, sees a one-line explanation of the effect and its blast radius ("Engineers will be able to cancel their own jobs"), saves.
  4. Change takes effect on the next request; affected nav pills appear/disappear; an audit row records actor, role, action key, before, after, timestamp.
  5. Admin cannot break the safeguards in §2.5; those cells are disabled with a reason.

End state: access policy is an office decision, not a release.

W9 — A user changes how the app looks

  1. Any signed-in user clicks the Aa button (same control as our published guides).
  2. Panel offers System / Light / Dark, type size, line height, layout width, font stacks and colour tokens; changes preview live.
  3. Saving stores the preferences on the user record, so the look follows them to any device.
  4. Admin sets the company default theme (used for users who never customise).
  5. Only design tokens change; layout, permissions and content never do.

End state: comfortable reading for a 55-year-old installer in sunlight and an office manager in a dark room, without a second stylesheet.

W10 — Find things and see the plan

  1. Global search box in the header: type a job reference, customer name, postcode, engineer name, or invoice number → grouped results, each opening the right record.
  2. Insights → Timeline shows a horizontal week/month view of jobs by planned dates, filtered by engineer and status.
  3. Insights → Workload shows jobs per engineer for a chosen week.
  4. Insights → Financials (Manager/Admin) shows quoted vs invoiced vs paid, with a date range.

End state: "what is happening next week and with whom" answered in one screen.

5. Screens and navigation

  • Top bar (few horizontal items only): brand, global search, Dashboard, Jobs, Customers, Insights, Admin (only if the user has any admin permission), user menu (name, appearance, change password, sign out).

5.3 Empty, error and edge states (must be designed, not left blank)

  • Empty list: one sentence explaining what would appear, plus the primary create action (only if the user may create).
  • Permission denied: a plain explanation and a "back to my work" link — never a raw 403 page.
  • Not found / archived: say it exists but is archived, with the reference.
  • Save success: inline confirmation near the action, plus the changed value visible; no silent saves.
  • Validation failure: field-level message in plain language, existing input preserved, focus moved to the first problem.
  • Session expiry: return to sign-in with an explanation; the form is not lost where avoidable (draft job data in the browser for create forms only).

6. UX and accessibility requirements

These are testable requirements, not aspirations.

# Requirement
U1 Reading text defaults to 25px; app interface text is never below 18px and no text is below 16px at default zoom.
U2 Primary buttons are at least 48px tall, with a minimum 44×44px hit area everywhere.
U3 Forms are single column, labels above fields, no placeholder-only labels.
U4 One primary action per screen; secondary actions are visually quieter pills.
U5 Common tasks fit a three-step budget: from sign-in to updating a status is ≤ 3 taps/links.
U6 Status is always shown as word + colour pill (not colour alone).
U7 Contrast meets WCAG 2.2 AA (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text) in both light and dark modes.
U8 Every interactive element is reachable by keyboard with a visible focus ring; the Aa panel closes on Escape.
U9 Works from 360px wide upwards; no horizontal page scroll; tables scroll inside their own container.
U10 Dates are unambiguous (Tue 17 Mar 2026, 09:00) and honour the company timezone.
U11 Language is plain and active ("Complete job", not "Submit"); no jargon on customer screens.
U12 Destructive or irreversible actions (cancel job, void invoice, archive) ask for confirmation that names the record.
U13 Loading states are visible within 300ms for any action that takes longer; no double-submits.
U14 Respects prefers-reduced-motion; no animation is required to understand a state change.
U15 Print stylesheets hide navigation, filters and the Aa panel; job sheet and invoice print on A4 with page numbers.

7. Visual design, theming and the guide system

7.1 House style

The app, this plan and the generated PDFs share the design system already used by the mini-js-coder explainers and book — warm paper, terracotta accent, serif headings, sans interface text, mono for references and code.

  • Palette sources: jms_theme.toml (the house tokens, light and dark) and the vendored skills/make-jms-html generator. The app and the guide pages share these values.
  • Typography: serif display for page and section titles; system sans for controls and prose; mono for job references, invoice numbers and dates in tables.
  • Type defaults (docs and app shell): reading text 25px, sidebar, contents and chrome 20.4px — exactly 20% larger than the 17px UI base our guides shipped with. Every other size is a multiplier of those two bases, so the page scales coherently; the Aa panel exposes both as sliders and the theme file stores them.
  • Surfaces: ivory cards on paper, 1px --line borders, 12–16px radii, soft shadows; no gradients, no heavy borders, no competing accent colours.
  • Do not fork the palette per screen. One set of CSS custom properties, scoped page overrides only.

7.2 Appearance ("Aa") panel — parity with the guides

The in-app appearance panel must behave like the one shipped in our generated book pages:

  • Round Aa button fixed top-right; panel opens directly below it.
  • Exactly three colour modes: System, Light, Dark; System resolves through prefers-color-scheme and writes the resolved mode to html[data-theme] so sidebar and content switch together.
  • Controls for Type (--fs-content, --lh-content, --fs-ui), Layout (--sidebar, --measure), Fonts (curated dropdowns for serif, sans, mono) and every colour token, grouped by Light/Dark.
  • Font choices are <select> dropdowns, never free text; an unknown saved stack survives as a Custom option.
  • Reset restores company defaults; Copy theme exports the current values as TOML in the same key names as book_theme.toml, so a user’s favourite look can be pasted into the company theme file.
  • State persists on the user record (not only in the browser) and re-applies before first paint to avoid a flash of the wrong theme.
  • Panel is hidden in print and on small screens; it never affects permissions, layout structure or content.

7.3 Company default theme

An Admin can set the default mode, type scale and palette in Company settings. Resolution order: user preference → company default → built-in house theme.

9. Security, audit and data protection

# Requirement
S1 Passwords hashed with a modern adaptive algorithm; default JMS123 is only a starting value and must be changed at first sign-in.
S2 Sessions expire after inactivity (default 60 minutes) and are revoked on password change, role change or deactivation.
S3 Authorisation is checked server-side on every request and every partial; object scoping per §2.4 is tested, not assumed.
S4 CSRF protection on all state-changing requests; the app never trusts hidden form fields for role or ownership.
S5 Login rate-limited; repeated failures logged with IP and user agent.
S6 Every job status change, assignment change, price change, permission change, invoice action and user change writes an audit record (actor, when, before, after).
S7 Customer-visible notes and internal notes are different fields with different visibility; the UI states which is which.
S8 Backups: daily database backup with a documented restore drill; restore is verified before go-live.
S9 Archiving replaces deletion for jobs and customers; invoices are never deleted, only voided.
S10 Personal data is limited to what the job needs; a customer can view their own contact details and ask the office to correct them (v1: no self-service edit).
S11 No secrets in the repository; configuration comes from environment variables (.env locally, host config in production).

15. Glossary

Term Meaning
Job One piece of work for one customer at one site, from draft to paid.
Assignment The link between a job and an engineer, with a lead flag.
Pill A rounded navigation/action control in the left table of contents.
Action key A stable permission string such as job.assign.
Scoping Filtering rows by the signed-in user's customer or assignment, not by the URL.
Actual vs planned Planned dates are promised; actual dates are what happened.
Gapless numbering Invoice numbers have no missing values; voids stay in the sequence.
Aa panel The floating appearance customiser shared with our published guides.
House style Warm paper, terracotta accent, serif headings — the guide design system.

Reminder of scope. JMS v1 is a small-business job tracker. It is not a production-grade platform for thousands of users, and it makes no claims about compliance beyond the practices listed in §9. Anything not in §1.3 is out of scope until a later plan says otherwise.
